Digital paradigms are the successive frameworks through which computation has been imagined and used: the mainframe, the personal computer, the browser, the mobile device, the cloud, the platform, the model. Each paradigm carries its own metaphors (desktop, page, feed, stream), its own economies, and its own aesthetics, shaping what music and art can be made and heard within it. Studying them as paradigms, rather than as neutral tools, reveals how deeply interfaces and infrastructures condition creative practice on the internet.
