The "Gang of Four" (GoF) are Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson, and John Vlissides, authors of Design Patterns (1994), the book that catalogued 23 reusable solutions to recurring problems in object-oriented software: Singleton, Observer, Factory, Strategy, and others. Beyond engineering, the GoF gave programming a pattern language in the sense of Christopher Alexander: named, shareable forms that describe relations rather than code. For internet music, patterns like Observer, in which objects subscribe to events, read as compositional figures of listening and response.

**"Gang of Four" (GoF)**, the authors of the classic book _"Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software"_.

In software engineering, "GoF" usually means:

- Erich Gamma
- Richard Helm
- Ralph Johnson
- John Vlissides

Their book catalogues 23 fundamental object-oriented design patterns (like Singleton, Observer, Factory Method, Strategy, etc.) and is a cornerstone reference in OOP and design patterns.
